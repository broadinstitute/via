Why use VIA?

No coding required

A purpose-built interface over All of Us genomic data. Paste variants, pick a phenotype and read the results, with no notebooks or SQL.

The full All of Us cohort

Look up any of more than a billion variants called from All of Us whole genomes, with frequencies overall and for each genetic ancestry group, beside gnomAD for reference.

Phenotype-matched comparison

Because every genome is linked to its participant's health records, VIA can find everyone recorded with a condition and show each variant's frequency among them next to the cohort-wide figure.

Stays inside your workspace

VIA runs as an app in your Researcher Workbench workspace and reads the curated data repository read-only. It stores no credentials of its own.

Two views of the same evidence

Scan every candidate in one table, then open Review to weigh one variant at a time.

Every candidate in one row

Each variant's frequency across all participants sits right beside its frequency among the phenotype-matched, so the comparison that matters takes a glance.

  • All of Us and gnomAD allele frequencies, with the ancestry group where each variant is most common
  • Homozygous and heterozygous carriers among matched participants, and ClinVar pathogenic variants in trans
  • ClinVar classification with review stars, SpliceAI scores and LOFTEE loss-of-function calls
  • Expand any row for the variant's frequency in each ancestry group

A verdict, and the evidence for it

Review compares matched participants with the rest of the cohort, one variant at a time, and says plainly what the numbers support.

  • A verdict for each variant: enriched, depleted, similar or inconclusive
  • Fisher's exact test, with a correction for testing many variants at once
  • An exact odds-ratio interval that holds up at the small counts typical of rare disease
  • Observed against expected carriers, adjusted for the matched group's ancestry
  • Candidates grouped by verdict and ranked by strength of signal, so the strongest leads come first

How does VIA work?

From a list of candidate variants to a short list worth a closer look.

  1. Open VIA in your workspace

    Launch VIA from a Researcher Workbench workspace with Controlled Tier access, which is where All of Us genomic data lives. There's nothing to install or configure.

    VIAVariant Interpretation Open

  2. Enter candidate variants

    Paste them one per line as chromosome, position, reference and alternate allele, the way they come out of most pipelines.

    19-11116928-G-A
    2-21006288-C-T
    1-55039974-G-T
    19-11120436-C-T

  3. Add a phenotype Optional

    Start typing a condition and pick it from the list. VIA counts the participants recorded with it and shows their breakdown by ancestry and age.

    hypercholest Familial hypercholesterolemia~386 Hypercholesterolemia~12,800

  4. Compare in the table

    Read each variant's frequency across all participants beside its frequency among matched participants, with annotations alongside.

    All participants0.0003 → Phenotype-matched0.0064

  5. Review the evidence

    Open Review on any variant for a verdict, the statistics behind it and its ancestry context. Export the table as TSV to share.

    Enriched Similar Inconclusive Depleted

Questions

The ones we're asked first. Details on the statistics are in the app itself, beside each figure.

Who can use VIA?

Researchers with Controlled Tier access to the All of Us Researcher Workbench. All of Us makes its genomic data available only in the Controlled Tier, and VIA reads that data, so Registered Tier access isn't enough. VIA runs as an app inside your workspace, and nothing leaves the Workbench.

How do I get Controlled Tier access?

Four steps, all through All of Us rather than VIA:

  1. Institutional agreement. Your institution needs a Data Use and Registration Agreement with All of Us that includes the Controlled Tier.
  2. Register. Create a Researcher Workbench account and verify your identity, which needs an eRA Commons account.
  3. Train and sign. Complete the Registered Tier training, sign the Data User Code of Conduct, then complete the additional Controlled Tier training.
  4. Institutional sign-off. Your institution approves your Controlled Tier access.

The All of Us guide to accessing genomic data has the current details and the list of institutions with agreements in place.

Do I need to write code?

No. You paste variant IDs, optionally pick a condition, and read the results. There are no notebooks, queries or files to manage.

What does VIA compare?

For each candidate variant, how often it appears across all All of Us participants with genomes, and, if you add a phenotype, how often it appears among the participants recorded with that condition. gnomAD frequencies, ClinVar classifications, SpliceAI scores and LOFTEE calls sit beside those figures for context.

How does VIA decide a variant is enriched or depleted?

Review compares the phenotype-matched participants with the rest of the cohort using Fisher's exact test and an exact confidence interval for the odds ratio. The verdict comes from that interval: enriched if it sits entirely above 1, depleted if entirely below, similar if it rules out a two-fold difference either way, and inconclusive otherwise. The p-value is also shown corrected for the number of variants you searched.

Is VIA a clinical tool?

VIA is a research tool. It summarises frequencies and statistics to support interpretation; it doesn't classify variants or make clinical determinations. Those remain with the interpreter, following your institution's processes.

What does VIA store?

Nothing on its own. It reads the curated data repository read-only using your workspace's credentials and keeps no copy. Your recent searches, just the variant IDs and the condition you picked, are remembered in your browser so you can re-run them, and you can clear them at any time.

How many variants can I search at once?

Up to 50 per search. You can export the results table as a TSV file to keep or share.
